# Decisions Log

Decisions made autonomously while the owner was away. Each can be revisited.

- **D-001 Stack**: TypeScript + Vite + three.js (WebGL2) + React for UI overlays. WebGL2 over WebGPU for laptop/browser compatibility.
- **D-002 Original IP**: All parts, art, names original. Agency name in-game: "Web Space Program". Crew are humans ("astronauts"), not a mascot species.
- **D-003 Scale**: Real data is the source; `s` scales lengths, GM by s² (preserves surface gravity), the atmosphere profile is compressed vertically by s^0.3 (standard → Earth atmosphere ≈ 70 km, like the genre classic), rotation periods by s^0.5. Default preset `standard` s=0.1. `real` preset uses exponent 1 for everything.
- **D-004 Rigid vessels**: Vessels are single rigid bodies (no wobbly joints). Structural failure via load thresholds. Removes KSP's "Kraken" class of bugs.
- **D-005 Gravity**: Patched conics for prediction and on-rails; physics mode uses dominant-body gravity, with optional n-body perturbations later.
- **D-006 Launch site**: Cape Canaveral-ish latitude (28.5°N) on Earth, east coast. Named "Coastal Launch Complex".
- **D-007 Autopilot building blocks** (`src/game/autopilot.ts`): planners (circularize, transfer to child/parent body, course correction) add maneuver nodes; controllers (ascent, node execution, powered landing) return a `ControlInput` per frame plus an optional `coastUntil` UT so any driver (tests now, an in-game autopilot later) can rails-warp idle phases. Node burns track remaining dv from measured proper acceleration and, for prograde/retrograde burns, stop on the planned orbital energy (finite-burn losses otherwise wreck near-escape burns like TLI). Landing = predictive suicide burn + slope-aware terminal descent (diverts to ground under ~8°). `tests/world-moon.test.ts` flies the stock Selene lander Earth → Moon landing → back into Earth's SOI with no balance changes.
- **D-008 Terrain relief**: relief scales by s^0.8 (standard: 0.16×) — slightly exaggerated vs radii for drama, but lunar slopes stay landable.
- **D-009 Tracking Station warp**: tracking uses the existing active-vessel warp clamps and encounter stops through `World.tick`; fleet focus is independent of the simulation's active vessel. With no active vessel, `advanceRailsTo` packs and propagates unattended vessels using existing rails event rules. Fleet conics refresh at 2 Hz while icon positions and floating-origin transforms update every frame.
- **D-010 Interplanetary targeting**: `src/game/interplanetary.ts` searches a scaled-ephemeris Lambert departure/flight-time grid, minimizes estimated ejection plus circular capture cost (capture optional), resolves the actual parking-orbit ejection plane and phase, and refines time/prograde/normal/radial against Earth → Sun → target patched conics. Prograde transfers with no complete extra revolutions only; exactly collinear Lambert endpoints are rejected as plane-ambiguous. Moon departure requires first reaching a planetary parking orbit. Ringrunner uses existing chemical engines and tanks, with no part balance changes; small late cruise trims are required because finite burns leave sub-m/s errors that matter over years. `porkchopGrid` exposes pure data for future UI work. The synchronous planner currently takes a few seconds; a worker is deferred.
- **D-011 Crew / EVA**: Crew are original human astronauts, generated from a persisted integer seed. Roster IDs live on command `PartState`s so staging and docking UID remaps preserve assignments. Catalog pod dry mass includes occupied seats (100 kg per suited astronaut); empty seats subtract that mass, preserving stock launch balance. EVA uses a runtime-only `eva-astronaut` part (95 kg dry + 5 kg monopropellant) and a dedicated DOM-free character step in the existing vessel/rails system. Hatch transfer preserves combined COM and linear momentum, with no separation impulse. Ground speed is 1.4 m/s walking / 3.5 m/s running, hop 2.5 m/s, slope limit 45°. Jetpack thrust is 35 N with 600 m/s exhaust velocity (~30.8 m/s total Δv). Boarding within 3 m of a free hatch and below 3 m/s relative speed services/refills the suit from abstract cabin supplies. Enter boards, leaving B available for aircraft brakes. Flags are body-fixed surface objects. Empty pods without a probe core accept no flight control or staging. Saves without crew data receive deterministic launch-style assignments on load.
- **D-012 Aircraft**: jets are `EngineModule`s with `airBreathing` (thrust = static thrust × Mach curve × (ρ/1.225)^k × intake-air fraction; fuel-only Isp, liquid fuel only). Intake air is produced every step and never stored (leftovers vented), so starvation at altitude/low speed is immediate; jets flame out below 15 % supply and relight automatically. Wheels are spring/damper suspensions with an impulse tyre model (lateral grip with sticky/sliding μ, rolling resistance, brakes inside a friction circle); steering fades with speed. Editor core now uses the catalogue surface-mount convention (srf node / −Z face); SPH crafts build horizontally (view-only rotation, craft +Y stays the nose) and launch on the runway (`World.launch(craft, 'runway')`, free on their wheels, no clamps).
- **D-013 Science / career**: `World.mode` defaults to `sandbox`; optional `SavedWorld.mode/progression` preserve version-1 saves. The executor's cut-off tail uses `spoolDownTime` (a rocket cut now ends in ~0.1 s), simulated with the engine's own discrete decay.
- **Lost control after assists (owner bug):** the assist could hand back a vessel still packed on rails warp (a "warp to" left running when the program started, or a program ending/failing without owning warp). A packed craft ignores throttle, attitude and SAS until a command or staging drops warp, which is why toggling gimbal lock "fixed" it. `flight/assist.ts assistFrame` now owns warp for the whole program (rails warp only while coasting, 1× on every other frame and at any end), passes the pilot's part commands/RCS/gear/brakes through, and hand-back enables SAS attitude hold. `World.tick` drops rails warp when the pilot steers or throttles a live engine (staging already did). Directional SAS holds yield per axis to pilot input (a saturated hold summed with the input cancelled it); a maneuver hold with no node falls back to attitude hold. `isTyping` no longer counts switches, sliders and buttons as text fields (a focused part-menu switch silenced every flight key); HUD selects blur after a pick. Regression: `tests/assist-handback.test.ts`.
- **Node executor:** turns to the burn at 1× before warping (rails keeps attitude); starts when half the Δv is delivered by the node time, from the per-stage Δv readout plus 1 s per staging; stages whenever nothing can thrust (never below `minStage` or into a parachute stage); steers with Lambert guidance to a quarter-turn aim point on the planned orbit; cuts on the exact planned orbital energy (quadratic, not linearised) with a simulated discrete spool-down tail. One physics step at minimum throttle is ≈0.3 m/s on the Selene booster (≈±25 km at the Moon after a TLI), so the main cut lands a step early and is trimmed with the engines' thrust limiters set temporarily (e.g. 6%); `Autopilot.release()` restores the player's limiter values however the program ends (done, failed, pilot override), and `runAutopilot` applies them too. A finite-burn TLI from the stock Selene orbit scenario (three start times) ends <0.5 m/s from the plan with the lunar periapsis within 6.3 km of planned (energy now exact; the rest is finite-burn direction) (`tests/assist-node.test.ts`; before: no staging until 34 s past the node, periapsis 819 km).
- **Departure options:** `game/planning/windows.ts` scans Lambert departures from the vessel's actual orbit in any plane (moons: one lunar month; returns home: half a month, any return plane and radial speed). The cheapest within the next revolution is the "now" option (usually a plane change); later local minima are windows. `planTransferToBody` takes the earliest option within 8% + 25 m/s of the cheapest (the first-window planner when it qualifies, else a Lambert-seeded patched-conic refinement), returns the options in `TransferPlan.windows`, and `options.departure` plans a specific one. The maneuver planner lists them (wait, Δv, flight time, soonest/cheapest/plane-change tags).
- **Interplanetary:** the grid now scores the real single-burn ejection from the fixed parking plane (min over the revolution) instead of a coplanar departure, which had picked windows needing 2–2.5× the Hohmann Δv from inclined orbits; options over two synodic periods rank departure + capture like the planner.
- **Matrix:** `PLANNER_MATRIX=1 npx vitest run tests/planner-matrix.test.ts` runs 135 requests (three scales; LEO at 0/28.5/51.6/90/140°, MEO, elliptical, part-burnt, overshot; lunar returns; Mars/Venus) and judges Δv against the best achievable single burn, waits against earlier near-equal options, encounter periapsis and the map's prediction.

## Control responsiveness (2026-10-07)

- **D-THROTTLE Rocket throttle response**: liquid rocket engines follow their `spoolTime` only for the start-up transient after ignition (`EngineState.spooledUp`, optional, reset whenever the engine is not ignited). Once running, throttle changes and cuts use `ROCKET_THROTTLE_TIME` = 0.02 s, so a full-throttle cut ends thrust in 0.1 s (previously the spool lag kept thrust for 4.6 × spoolTime, e.g. 4.6 s on the 1.0 s lifter, and Ap kept rising for 2–3 s after the player cut). Jets keep their turbine spool time in both directions; solids are unchanged. The node executor's cutoff anticipation uses `spoolDownTime`. The map path caches (`trajectory()`, `mapTrajectory()`) refresh at once when thrust ends instead of keeping a prediction made under thrust for the 2 s coasting age. Any pilot throttle input (cut/full keys, ramp keys, analog trigger) now cancels a running flight assist, as manual attitude input already did; before, the assist overwrote a throttle cut every frame.
- **D-MAPBURN Map path while burning**: under thrust the map draws the current-SOI conic recomputed from the live state every frame (`World.burnTrajectory`, ~0.1–0.4 ms) plus only the next continuation; `trajectory()` computes just those two patches while thrusting (5 Hz). Deeper continuations (re-encounters after a lunar escape and the like) change branch with every m/s and flickered between refreshes; they return within one frame of the engines stopping. A cached continuation whose start no longer matches the live conic's end (e.g. the escape just closed) is dropped until the next refresh. `MapView` rebuilds only the lines whose patch changed. Precise-gravity (numerical) map paths are unchanged. Probe: `scripts/map-burn-probe.ts`.
- **D-HUBLOAD Space Center first load**: the hub scene compiles its programs in slices (as flight does), updates without drawing until then, and keeps its canvas transparent behind a "Preparing the launch complex…" label until Earth's surface is ready (12 s cap), then fades in over 0.45 s. `PlanetRenderer.surfaceReady` no longer reports ready while the untextured far sphere stands in for wanted near terrain (also tightens the flight loading gate). Cold new-career load on the RTX harness: before, content from ~2.5 s after Start with ~1.5 s of white ground and pop-in, settled ~5 s; after, nothing until a single fade-in at ~4–4.5 s on the settled scene.

## GPU pass 2 (2026-10-07, cx/perfgpu2)

- **Aerial integration follows density variation on Low/Medium.** Short, nearly level daylight rays use one or two integration segments when their radial span, including chord sag, is small relative to the aerosol scale height. Exponential segment integration is exact for constant density; this is an approximation when density varies. Sunset/night, long horizon rays, optically thick step multipliers, High and Ultra keep the original budget. Airless bodies return before atmospheric ray calculations. Geometry, imagery, terrain and ocean fragment shading are unchanged. Paired measurements and visual comparisons are recorded in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`.
- **Only certainly hidden Earth sky rays are discarded.** Logarithmic fragment depth prevents ordinary early depth rejection from avoiding the sky march. A CPU Float64 bound places an occluding sphere below the global terrain minimum, triangle sag and a precision margin. Rays through that sphere cannot show sky behind the complete opaque Earth surface, including launch-trench floors. Apply the shortcut only within two Earth radii: distant Float32 ray discriminants lose precision, and those small discs provide little benefit. The horizon band retains the original shader. Other bodies retain the original sky path.
- **Flight terrain/ocean use reusable batches when multi-draw is available.** Preserve the existing chunk vertices, attributes, per-chunk culling and rebased transforms; combine each material family into a `BatchedMesh`. Reserve GPU slots for the peak selected chunks and reuse them through LOD changes, rather than retaining GPU buffers for the whole terrain cache. Matrices are computed after Float64 floating-origin subtraction before their Float32 texture upload. Low and browsers without `WEBGL_multi_draw` retain ordinary meshes; hub/map/portraits also retain their existing path. This adds a matrix texture fetch and some reserved buffer capacity in exchange for fewer draw submissions. No shader precision reduction is assumed safe without Apple hardware evidence.
- **Medium clouds skip detail noise only at zero baked coverage.** Noise multiplies coverage, so this branch returns the same zero. Evaluate footprint derivatives before branching to preserve cloud-edge filtering. Restrict the shortcut to Medium; High/Ultra keep their original branch structure and Low disables clouds. Three interleaved pad pairs improve 2.530→2.493 ms.
- **Keep equivalent shader expressions without attributing an unproven speedup.** Terrain colour/coverage and GB slope reuse the same triplanar fetches, preserving the original cyclic accumulation order. The positive Mie denominator uses `x * sqrt(x)` for `pow(x, 1.5)` without changing the scattering model. Isolated RTX pad medians are essentially equal (fetch sharing 2.529→2.527 ms; phase rewrite 2.494→2.496 ms). These make the intended reuse/algebra explicit to compilers; Apple performance remains unmeasured. The wider terrain/ocean/shadow math experiment regressed and was removed.

## CPU pass 2, round 2 (2026-10-08, cx/perfcpu2)

- **Terrain chunk size:** every quality preset doubles `chunkResolution` (Low 48, Medium/High 64, Ultra 96). Selection still splits on each chunk's vertex spacing, so screen error stays at or below the preset target and `maxLevel` drops by one automatically. The pad selects ~200 instead of ~535 chunks: draw calls, not triangles, dominate the frame on the CPU. Triangle count rises modestly, per-chunk worker builds take ~4× longer and the LRU caches were cut to about a quarter so memory stays similar. Revisit with the GPU pass if vertex cost on Apple GPUs turns out to matter more.
- **Static containers:** the flight `Scene`, `PlanetRenderer.root` and the quadtree group are `matrixAutoUpdate = false` (they never move), so their per-frame `updateMatrix()` no longer forces a matrix multiply on every descendant, including hundreds of hidden cached chunks. The flight scene also has `matrixWorldAutoUpdate = false`; the controller calls `scene.updateMatrixWorld()` once per frame before rendering. Anything that transforms one of these containers must call `updateMatrix()` itself.
- **Shadow map cache:** `render/core/shadowCache.ts` sets `shadow.autoUpdate = false` on the sun and requests a redraw only when a visible caster that can reach the light's volume (conservative cylinder test) moved by more than a quarter shadow texel (rotation scaled by the caster's reach), changed geometry/instances, appeared or disappeared, or the light camera or map changed. Pad and attitude-hold orbit skip ~90–95% of depth passes; ascent still redraws every frame.
- **Rigid batching of animated parts:** (superseded by rigid node slots, 2026-10-09 op/vesselbatch, below) `batchStaticParts` takes a `skip` predicate; `isAnimatedNode` lists every node flight animates or restyles (gimbal, exhaust anchors, deploy data, gear joints, brace stages, control surfaces, lamp lenses, EVA limbs). Everything else in engines, panels, pods and gear joins the vessel batch. EVA vessels and the stress overlay keep individual meshes. A new animation in `VesselView.animatePart` must add its node name there.
- **Ephemeris:** caches states for four recent times (LRU) instead of one; the frame interleaves physics, interpolated render and world times. Values are unchanged.
- **Terrain selection reuse:** the quadtree reuses the previous selection while the body-fixed camera position (1 cm), orientation (1e-6), projection and chunk set (version bumped on attach/dispose) are unchanged, and then skips eviction.
- **Near-radial conics:** `stateToElements` derives a hyperbola's mean anomaly from the state (e·sinh F = r·v / √(μ|a|)) instead of from ν. On a radial approach ν lies within rounding of an asymptote and the conversion threw, unmounting flight.
- **Autosave persistence:** flight autosaves snapshot (`serializeWorld`) synchronously, then stringify, write the slot index and write the legacy key as three separate idle tasks (`requestIdleCallback`, 1 s timeout). Every save read, other save and `pagehide`/hidden visibility flush the queue first, so ordering and visibility are unchanged; a failed step reports once and skips the rest of that save. Quicksaves and hub/tracking saves stay synchronous. The slot index caches each slot's JSON while storage still holds exactly the text it last wrote, so a write stringifies only the new slot; any outside change (another tab, import) re-parses. Save format and keys are unchanged.
- **Imagery streaming budget:** replaces the fixed 0.75 ms / four-operation budget, which at 4× throttle admitted one 1 MiB unit per frame and tripled stream time. Each frame now spends about 6% of the measured frame interval (1–3 ms), admits a unit only if its predicted cost fits, and starts allocation/mip-generation units only at the beginning of a frame. Worker decoding and 1 MiB strips stay.
- **Not changed, by choice:** packed/prelaunch hazard stepping and stage-Δv readout cadence (both would change simulation or readout results), React HUD memoisation (≈0.4 ms real at a 10 Hz tick, spread thinly) and the navball's separate WebGL context.

- **Maneuver node anchored during its burn (op/mnvexec):** a node's planned orbit used to be re-derived from the live vessel every refresh (`live orbit at node.time + whole node Δv`), so it ran away from the target while burning, and it vanished the moment the node time passed. `ManeuverNode.anchor` (optional, persisted with the node) now freezes the planned post-burn state (body-relative position + velocity at the node time). `game/world/nodeAnchors.ts` keeps a fresh pre-burn plan for the next node (≤ 1 h ahead, every 0.5 s) and promotes it to `node.anchor` on the first engine thrust inside the burn window (`node.time − (burn + 30 s)` … `node.time + 1.5·burn + 60 s`, burn = Δv/(F/m)). Edits to the node (any field, time) or an SOI change invalidate the anchor (`validAnchor`), so editing re-plans from the live orbit as before. `predictTrajectory` continues from the anchored state at the node; once the node time has passed the prediction is a zero-length patch of the live orbit (drawn as its full ellipse, so it converges onto the plan) followed by the plan starting at the node time. Remaining Δv = planned velocity − the live orbit's velocity at the node time (`remainingManeuverDv`, shared by the navball burn readout, the navball/SAS maneuver direction and the node executor, which therefore resumes a burn already under way correctly). The navball maneuver marker now points along the remaining Δv in the inertial frame at the node, not the node components in the live orbit's frame. An anchored node survives the 600 s expiry while thrusting and is cleared (message "Maneuver complete") once the engines have been quiet for 2 s and the remaining Δv is ≤ max(0.25 m/s, 0.5 % of the node) (cap 2 m/s); an early cut leaves it in place. Precise-gravity (numerical) map paths still re-derive nodes from the live state, as before.

## WP2 editor (2026-10-08, op/wp2)

- **Autostaging over decoupled assemblies:** `autoStage` builds the tree of pieces between decouplers. A stack-decoupled child is a lower (serial) stage: its engines burn, then its decoupler fires in its own stage, then the parent's engines ignite (identical to the old depth stager for serial stacks; a test checks every stock craft without radial decouplers). A radially decoupled child (side latch or surface-mounted decoupler) is parallel: it ignites with its parent and drops in the next stage, so core + boosters light together. Each decoupler fires in the stage that drops its own assembly; engineless side pieces drop just before their parent; fairings open with their assembly's engines (or right after launch on the first stage); chutes fire last. We kept "decoupler, then next engines" as separate stages rather than hot staging: it matches the tuned separation impulses/throttle-cut behaviour (D-feel staging) and the depth stager, so stock designs stage the same.

## WP6 handling with inertia (2026-10-08, cx/wp6)

- **Actuators:** cap combined two-axis gimbal travel at 25°/s, including reversals and return after locking. Preserve D-THROTTLE's liquid ignition spool-up and 0.1 s running-engine cuts; reset the ignition transient on explicit shutdown/reignition. No thrust/Isp/catalogue changes.
- **SAS and coast wheels:** retain the full-inertia/gyroscopic feed-forward and actual torque-per-inertia braking envelope. ## Flight assist QA fixes (2026-10-08, op/assistfix)

- **Landing stage.** Before the deorbit burn, Land picks the first allowed stage in firing order whose engines at minimum throttle weigh at most 95% of the craft at its estimated touchdown mass, needing thrust-to-weight ≥ 1.3 and ideally the landing Δv (1.15·√(v²+2gh)+30 m/s). If no stage hovers outright, it falls back to one whose minimum is at most 4 landing weights. The terminal phase pulses that minimum, and engine spool smooths the pulses; the challenging/real Foehn landers at 2.5–3.3× still touch down at ~1.5 m/s. Land stages down in orbit (engines idle, 4 s separation), so a full Selene drops its Chinook (≈8×) and Zephyr and lands on the Doldrum. It refuses up front with the reason when no allowed stage qualifies, or when the stages to drop contain parachutes. `StageDeltaV.minThrustVac` supplies the throttle floor.
- **Truthful touchdown.** Losing parts at or after contact (or losing the vessel) is an error, e.g. "crashed · 18.2 m/s · 3 parts lost". Tipping over after settling is also an error. Braking never plans below the minimum throttle.
- **Landing coast warp.** The braking start is solved once by bisection along the coasting conic, using the same constant-throttle braking predictor from future coast states. Warp ends 15 s before that start, and the 2 Hz trigger at 1× still decides the burn. Status counts down ("coast · braking burn in 2:14"). This cuts a 20 km lunar landing from ~400 to ~140 real seconds.
- **Node guidance.** The closed-loop aim point is the farthest point of the planned post-burn orbit, up to the planned patch's end (encounter/SOI change), 175°, 0.9 SOI or 0.45 T, instead of a quarter turn. A guided burn now cuts and trims on the Lambert remainder; energy matching applies only without guidance. A late start, such as Execute node after a quickload past the centred start, or a long low-TWR burn, now still arrives where the plan goes. Zephyr TLI: plan 30 km, before 104 km (clean) / impact (late), now 28–31 km. "N m/s off the plan" reports the guided remainder.
- **Alarms.** Nodes an Execute-node program flies are quiet (no "node in 30 s" alarm); cancelling or failing the program restores the reminder. Node alarms, fired ones included, disappear when their node is consumed or deleted. Reconciling also runs when the node array is replaced, so `nodes = []` plus a new node no longer leaves a duplicate. A node-alarm hold is acknowledged while an assist runs, and starting an assist acknowledges any hold, so an old ✓ alarm can no longer keep every later assist coast at 1×.

- **Load resumes the saved scene (2026-10-08, op/saveeva):** `SaveSlot.scene` ('flight' | 'spaceCenter', optional) records where a save was made. In-flight quicksaves/autosaves/"Save as…" are 'flight'; saves from the Space Center, editor, tracking (to hub) and the autosave written when leaving flight are 'spaceCenter'. ## Terrain and texture heap (2026-10-08, op/heapfix)

- **One index pattern.** Land and ocean chunks carry no index arrays; every chunk of a quadtree shares one
  `BufferAttribute` over `chunkIndices(res)`. Evicted geometries detach it before dispose so its GPU buffer survives.
- **Cache budget in bytes.** `terrain.cacheMB` (Low 16, Medium 32, High 64, Ultra 96) caps CPU bytes of *stale*
  chunks (not visited by the selection walk for 30 frames); `cacheSize` now counts land + ocean meshes. Chunks in
  use (selected, their ancestors and out-of-frustum siblings) are never evicted: on the pad they are the bulk of the
  cache, and only denser packing or Ultra density (owner's call) reduces them. Stale chunks are evicted during a
  reused selection too (only those the last walk did not visit) and while the planet is drawn far.
- **Own multi-draw batch instead of three's BatchedMesh.** BatchedMesh keeps a full JS copy of the batch and only
  doubles. `ChunkBatch` is a Mesh drawn through three's batching program path (`isBatchedMesh` + matrices/indirect
  textures) over GL buffers it owns (`GLBufferAttribute`): slots are written from the chunk arrays, resizes copy
  GPU-side, growth ×1.5, shrink to 1.5× the recent peak after 8 s at ≤ 40 % use, release when idle. Index buffers
  are typed as element data inside a private VAO; all writes use COPY_* targets. Depends on three internals
  (`_multiDraw*`, `_matricesTexture`, `_indirectTexture`): re-check on three upgrades (tests/chunk-batch.test.ts).
  The per-draw fallback without WEBGL_multi_draw (Firefox) uses the same fields.
- **Dropping uploaded pixels.** `render/texturePixels.ts`: owners drop DataTexture pixels once their renderer holds
  them; an upload that still needs them only allocates and `onUpdate` refills asynchronously. Flight drops the far
  bake and the part textures it uploaded; at flight end part textures revert to 1×1 placeholders and reload through
  the worker/IndexedDB path so editor/thumbnail renderers never upload empty pixels.

- **Vessel ground bounce and sea glint (2026-10-09, op/vessellight):** The flight sky environment's planet disc is tinted by the body's *darkest* palette colour (Earth's ocean, lunar maria at 0.055) with irradiance ≈ 1, so a hull landed on a bright, sunlit Moon received roughly a tenth of the light the visible regolith reflects, and its shadow side went black. An additive `uBounce` term in the existing PMREM bake (no new pass or program, and re-baked under the same quantised key) adds local ground radiance = albedo × 3.2/π (the hull's own sun irradiance) × the disc's Lambert term, × 1.3 regolith backscatter on airless bodies. Albedo comes from the mid palette colour on land and the darkest over liquid. It scales with eclipse visibility and the sun's colour, and fades between 0.3 % and 3 % of the radius above the datum, where the local albedo stops representing the disc. The shared water BRDF soft-compresses its sun glint to ≲ 4 (`s/(1+s/4)`) instead of hard-clamping at 200. Near the sea at grazing view, a wide glitter path sat at the clamp, and bloom smeared the low-sun orange over the frame as a brown veil (the "murky splashdown"; aerial perspective and camera height were ruled out by shader probes). Glints seen from orbit (≲ 1) are essentially unchanged.
- **Map markers under HUD panels are nudged clear (op/mapmarkers).** Option A: an orbit marker (Ap/Pe, encounter, escape, impact, AN/DN, closest approach) whose true point is within 6 px of a HUD block is drawn at the nearest spot ≥ 10 px outside every block and inside the viewport, joined to the true point by a 1 px leader line (65 % opacity, marker colour). A caption that would run under a block flips to the glyph's left (right-aligned via `right:`), and is dropped if that side is covered too (pinned details stay). HUD rectangles come from `HudRectCache` (`render/map/hudRects.ts`): a selector list (`flight/hud/hudBlocks.ts`, mirrors the `pointer-events: auto` rules in hud.css, plus `.nb-speed` and `.stg-dv` which overflow their parents) is measured with `getBoundingClientRect` only after a ResizeObserver / MutationObserver (child list, class/style) / window-resize signal, at most every 250 ms, never per marker; rows scrolled out of a clipping list are clipped to it. Per frame: no allocation except the leader's transform string while a nudged marker moves. Maneuver-node sprites and the gizmo are not nudged (a node under a panel can still be reached by moving the camera; the node menu lives in the HUD).

- **Terrain chunk vertices packed to 20 B (2026-10-09, op/vtxpack).** Positions are stored relative to the chunk's 1024 m-aligned origin instead of its centre: the local position then *is* the detail-texture coordinate, which drops `aDetail` (12 B), and the offset grows by at most ~1.8 km, so float32 stays sub-millimetre. `quadtree.place` translates by the origin; bounding spheres (and the batch frustum test, which now applies the slot matrix to the sphere centre) keep the true centre. ## Vessel draws once per material (2026-10-09, op/vesselbatch)

- **Rigid node slots.** Every merged vessel vertex carries `wspNode`; a shared 384×64 RGBA32F texture (`render/core/rigidNodes.ts`) holds each moving frame's 3×4 transform relative to the vessel frame. Moving frames are gimbals, control surfaces, gear joints, brace stages and every `userData.deploy` node (`isMovingNode`). Their leaves merge in the node's own frame, so a vessel draws once per material (and vertex layout) while engines gimbal and arrays deploy. Plumes (exhaust anchors), lamp lenses (per-part material) and EVA limbs stay individual. Slot 0 is the identity for rigid geometry and every unbatched mesh, so one program serves both. This supersedes the settled-deployable second batch and the per-node individual draws of `isAnimatedNode`.
- **Why a shared texture, not uniforms:** part materials are shared by every vessel, so a per-vessel uniform could not tell vessels apart (three only refreshes material uniforms when the material changes). Each vessel reserves whole rows of 128 slots (64 rows), so its changes upload as one `texSubImage2D` range per row. Out of rows → that vessel's moving frames draw individually (old behaviour).
- **Node transforms are rigid + uniform scale** (rotation, translation, the chute canopy's uniform scale), so the vertex shader maps normals with the 3×3 part; a hidden node (`visibleAbove`) gets a zero matrix (degenerate triangles). A new non-uniform-scale animation would need a normal matrix.
- **Node patch lives in the thermal part variant** (`thermal-v3`), and `thermalGeometry` always adds `wspNode` (an unbound attribute would read whatever generic value another program left at that location). One program family: no new programs to warm, and staging's unbatched frames use the same programs as the batch. Hand-written override shaders (photo DOF depth) use `NODE_POSITION_GLSL` with a default attribute value of 0.
- **Shadows:** merged meshes get a node-aware `customDepthMaterial` (one shared, plus one per alpha-tested source). The shadow cache cannot see shader-side motion, so pose changes over 1e-4 bump `userData.casterVersion`, which joins the caster key. Meshes with moving frames are `frustumCulled = false` (deployed geometry leaves the rest-pose bounds).
- **Only re-posed parts rewrite slots:** each part remembers gimbal a/b, gear compression/steer/spin and deploy; unchanged → no matrix work and no upload. A new animation input in `animatePart` must be added to that pose vector.
- **Repeats are flattened, not instanced.** An instanced draw needed its own `#inst` program variant and material setup, which cost more GL calls than its vertices. Selene gains ~12 k vertices (+12%); Selene-real ~+4 k. Multi-material leaves (fins, flaps) are split by geometry group instead of drawn individually. Supersedes the instancing part of "Batched part heat".
- **Packed ORM bound once:** flight part variants whose roughness/metalness/AO maps are the same texture drop the metalness and AO maps and read B and R from the roughness texel (three's own chunk math). This means two fewer texture binds per material switch and two fewer fragment fetches. It follows the variant's current maps: restoring them returns three's path (used for the same-frame A/B, pixel-identical).
- **Context restore:** the node texture is CPU-backed, so three re-uploads it, but pending update ranges would make that upload partial. Every renderer that compiles a node program subscribes to its restore; the callback clears ranges and forces one full upload; ranges resume after three's `onUpdate`.
  Verified in Firefox and Chrome with a forced loss: batched and individual meshes still match after the restore and after a post-restore re-pose (docs/PERFORMANCE.md).
- **Not done:** merging colour-only material variants (accent/anodised families) into vertex colour would save ~3 more draws on Selene; skipped for now (small, and clearcoat/normal parameters differ in most families).


## Planet shader clock wrap (2026-10-09, op/seaedge)
- **Cause of the stair-stepped glint edge near splashdown:** not cloud shadows, masks or depth. Flight passes absolute UT (~9.6e8 s) as `uTime`, and a float32 uniform at that size has a 64 s step. The ocean's wave drift `(vDetail + uTime·k) / scale` reached ~2e9, where float32 steps are 128 m, so wave normals were constant over 128 m squares aligned to the body axes. Inside the glint that showed as tilted blocks with stair-step edges (and, at 500 m, a checkerboard). The waves also only moved once a minute. This has been present since flight first used the planet renderer (`a5269c94`).
- **Fix:** `shaderTime()` (`render/planet/uniforms.ts`) folds the clock to 1e5 s on the CPU before upload (planet uniforms, the Sun, surface features). This gives 8 ms resolution. 1e5 s leaves the gas-giant `mod(t, 1e5)` unchanged and is a whole multiple of the 4000 s and 12500 s effect cycles; the other animations jump once per ~28 h of game time. LaunchSite already wraps its wind clock (`t % 4096`).
- **Visible result:** the sea near the camera now shows the designed wave glitter (sparkling glint path) instead of a flat sheen with blocks. Orbit views are unchanged (Chrome mean pixel difference 0.16/255).
- **Cost (Chrome fenced GPU, medium, A/B/A/B):** pad and orbit equal within noise. On the sea view the frame rises from ~2.44 to ~2.54 ms; almost all of that is `UpscalePass`, whose flat-footprint early-out no longer covers the now-detailed water (0.117 → 0.228 ms). The scene pass rises by +0.02 ms.
- **Rejected:** fading wave octaves by texture footprint. It hid part of the symptom; the blocks persisted with mipmapping disabled, and the real cause was the time precision.

## Blocky Moon after a context restore (2026-10-09, op/moonctx)

- **Cause: adaptive resolution, not lost GPU data.** Every Moon texture, chunk and batch slot came back after the restore (probes: near mode, same chunk count, batch draws = live slots). The blocky look is the *far sphere* (2048-px bake, ~2.7 km texels), and it also appeared before any loss: in the vtxpack "A" run the Moon was already blocky because adaptive resolution had dropped during loading. The near/far switch (`wantsTerrainLod`: projected radius > bake width / 5) used `lodViewportHeight`, which includes the adaptive scale. In a 20 km lunar orbit the projected radius sits close to that threshold, so a drop to 0.55 put the Moon on the far sphere. The restore triggers this because every program recompiles over a few seconds of slow (< 250 ms) frames, which adaptive treats as overload. It recovered only once adaptive climbed back, which Firefox (no GPU timer queries, frames rarely at the target) often never did.
- **Fix.** The near/far decision uses `RenderPipeline.lodSwitchViewportHeight` (`lodViewportHeight` with the adaptive factor removed; `switchViewportHeight` on the planet update, flight only). Chunk refinement (screen-space error) still follows the adaptive scale, so a GPU-bound drop still sheds geometry (42 instead of ~70 chunks at 0.5), but the body no longer swaps to the far bake. Behaviour at adaptive scale 1 is unchanged. Separately, `AdaptiveQuality.hold(5000)` runs on `webglcontextrestored`, so the recompile stall is not judged as load (sustained slowness afterwards still is). Hub, map and portraits keep a single height (no adaptive scaling there that matters).
- **Launch site after a restore (found while checking the pad, also pre-existing on main):** the trench walls and floor, the building facades and the gantry base disappeared for good (stars showing through the trench). `SiteTextureArray` layers only ever existed on the GPU (`copyTextureToTexture` from canvases), so three re-allocated the array empty and every textured site surface hit its alpha discard. The array now keeps its source map (the canvases are already held by the site texture cache and `LaunchSite.track`, so no extra memory) and a `RestoreHook` queues every layer again; the next site draw re-runs the normal `fill`.
- **Rejected:** hysteresis on the switch (it would still go to far after a large enough drop and needs tuning per body); re-uploading or re-baking Moon data (nothing was missing).
- **Verified (production build, forced `WEBGL_lose_context`; adaptive scale forced to 0.5 six seconds after the restore for a deterministic A/B):** Firefox: main went to far and stayed there (blocky); branch stayed near. CDP Chrome: main went to far while at 0.5 (back to near only when the scale recovered); branch stayed near. Unforced Firefox Moon, plus Earth 150 km orbit and the pad in Firefox and Chrome, stay near through the restore; the pad's site surfaces come back in both browsers (main: missing after 40 s). Driver `C:\wsp-tools\drv-ff\moonctx-ts.mjs`; shots `C:\wsp-tools\shots\moonctx\` (`ff-{base,fix}-force-*`, `cdp-{base,fix}-force-*`, `*-fix-{moon,orbit}-*`, pad `cdp-base-pad-*` vs `{cdp,firefox}-fix2-pad-*`). ## Regolith detail tiling (2026-10-09, op/moontile)
- **Cause of the diagonal grid on the Moon's day side (≲8 km, Medium and up):** the terrain detail texture, not the WP5 craters, rocks or dust. Its texture coordinate `vDetail` is chunk-local and repeats every 1024 m, and the coarse albedo octave tiled a 256 m copy of a 256² value-noise tile. That tile has strong large features (a dark "cross"), so 20+ repeats per view read as a grid running diagonally across the screen. Turning off only that octave's albedo removed the grid at 8 km and 2 km. At 40–200 m the 32 m and 4 m octaves showed a weaker regular blob lattice.
- **Fix (`createTerrainMaterial`, same 6 + 3 + 3 fetches as before):** the coarse pair samples absolute body-fixed metres (`transpose(uBodyRot) * rel`; float32 error ≤ 0.25 m, invisible at these scales) at prime periods 1531 m and 263 m instead of 1024/256 m. Each finer octave (263 m, 32 m, 4 m) blends between two independent channels of its own fetch (A↔R) under a region mask taken from the next coarser fetch, with a polynomial variance correction. Neighbouring tiles therefore differ and there is no long-range lattice. The 32 m and 4 m octaves stay on `vDetail` (no precision change near the ground).
- **Cost (Chrome / 3060 Ti, Medium, 1600×900, fenced, old and new shaders swapped in the same scene, 3 interleaved reps):** 8 km +0.004 ms, 2 km +0.006 ms, 200 m +0.007 ms, 40 m +0.010 ms. Projected to 2940×1840 that is ≤ ~0.04 ms. A direct measurement at that size was within its ±0.1 ms clock noise. Low has `detail: false` and is unchanged.
- **Rejected:** (1) Domain warp of the 256 m octave by the coarse fetch. It removed the grid but gave marbled or wood-grain streaks, and still +0.015 ms. (2) A second incommensurate 409 m octave (+3 fetches). It was clean but cost +0.02–0.03 ms at 1600×900, about +0.05–0.066 at 2940×1840. (3) The same without the 1531 m layer: the 263/409 lattices share axes and beat into a fine row pattern at 8 km. (4) Skipping triplanar planes with ~0 weight to pay for extra fetches: not reliably faster on ANGLE, sometimes slower.
- **Visible result:** the Moon's mottling looks the same in kind, with no grid at 8 km, 2 km, 200 m or 40 m (Chrome and Firefox). Earth's pad is unchanged. From 3 km Earth's green/brown mottling is a little less blocky. Shots: `C:\wsp-tools\shots\moontile\shot-{before,after}-*.png`, `ff-{before,after}-*.png`; driver `scripts/bench/moontile-shots.mjs`.

## Crater cut-offs, pad grating vs exhaust, deployed gear on EVA (2026-10-09, op/leftovers)
- **Cause of the cut-off crater rims (Mercury, also Moon/Mars):** the crater generator, not LOD seams or the detail shader. Each crater centre is hashed inside a 3D cell and then projected onto the unit sphere, which moves it radially by up to half a cell; a third of all craters end up outside their host cell. The search visited only cells adjacent to the query point (reach 1.45 radii), so wherever a crater's host was further away its contribution vanished along a cell plane: straight cliffs across big crater walls, rims and ejecta ending in a straight edge. About 0.3–0.6 % of surface points were wrong (vs a brute-force ±2-cell reference).
- **Fix (`CraterField.height`):** search the swept window that can hold a host (lateral reach 1.6 radii, which also stops the old ejecta clipping, plus 0.5·|x_i| per axis for the projection shift), with pre-sqrt rejects (radial slab, shell, lateral dot test). That alone cost +60 % per Mercury vertex, so each octave now keeps the craters of its last cell window: the first query in a new window tests cells directly; a second *different* point in the same window lists the window once and later queries reuse it; an identical repeat (colour after height) reuses the point's own list. Results are bit-identical to the reference in any query order (tests). Terrain changes only where craters had been cut. Existing saves and landmarks are unaffected except at those spots.
- **Cost (Node, `buildChunk` res 32 / `bakeEquirect` 512, ms):** Mercury L2 8.0→7.2, L6 6.9→5.5, L10 6.0→3.8, L14 5.8→3.0, L17 5.6→2.9, bake 845→775. Moon L2 5.4→6.4, L6 4.7→4.7, L10 3.9→3.0, L14 3.6→2.2, L17 3.6→2.1, bake 545→645. Mars L2 6.7→7.7, L6 5.5→5.7, L10 5.1→4.2, L14 4.6→2.8, L17 4.4→2.6, bake 665→800. Near-ground chunks (what streams during a landing) are 20–50 % cheaper; Moon/Mars coarse chunks and far-sphere bakes, where most octaves are finer than the sample spacing and miss the cache, cost +15–20 % worker time (Medium 1024 bake: ≈+0.4 s once per body). None of this is main-thread frame time. Rejected: dropping craters whose projected centre leaves the host cell (33 % of all craters, a visible change everywhere); a sheared column enumeration and a lateral cell cull (no measurable gain; ~11 hashed cells per octave is near the minimum for a correct search).
- **Pad grating vs exhaust: no change needed.** CDP Chrome, Meridian ignition → liftoff from side, along-trench and overhead views, against a build from before the grating (`41c9eeb5^`). The engine glow and exhaust core show between the bars (the bars catch the engine light), the deluge steam and trench billows are unchanged, the liftoff plume is identical, and there is no z-fighting at the hole edges or moiré from the tower camera. ## Invisible vessel on the second launch (2026-10-12, op/invisship)
- **Cause:** the vessel node-transform texture (`rigidNodes.ts`) tracked "already on the GPU" in one module flag, but GPU textures are per renderer and every flight creates a new renderer. Once the first flight's renderer had uploaded it, the next flight's vessel queued per-row update ranges before the new renderer had uploaded anything, and three applies pending ranges to a texture's first upload (`texStorage2D` + range `texSubImage2D` only). Slot 0 (the identity used by every rigid and unbatched hull vertex) stayed zero, so those triangles collapsed to a point. Parts under moving nodes (engine gimbals, deployable arrays) had their own slots in the uploaded ranges, and plumes/FX don't use node programs: they stayed visible, as reported. Any flight → editor/hub → flight did it; orbit, reentry and landing were incidental.
- **Fix:** a renderer's first node-program compile (`patchNodeVertex`, already tracked per renderer for context-restore subscriptions) now runs the same full-upload reset as a context restore: clear pending ranges, stop queueing ranges until the next upload, mark for update. Compiles always precede that renderer's first draw, both for prewarm (`compileInSlices`) and lazy compiles. Cost: one full 393 KB upload per new renderer; nothing per frame.
- **Evidence:** regression test in `tests/vessel-deploy-batch.test.ts` (fails on main). CDP Chrome and Firefox, Meridian on the pad, flight → editor → flight: on main the second launch shows only small bits of the vessel; with the fix it matches the first launch. ## Engine interstage skirts (2026-10-12, op/shroud)
- **Owner report:** an upper-stage bell hung bare between stages and sat on the next stage's taper ("there would be a casing around an engine that tapers in to make it look like one smooth solid rocket"). This is stock Selene on main: Zephyr → Snap Ring 1 → Taper 1-2 → 2.5 m booster.

## Stowed arrays and antennas, air-load tearing, extend above atmosphere (2026-10-12, op/panels)

Supersedes the "retractable solar arrays/antennas default extended" sentence of the part interaction layer entry.
- **Default stowed.** Tracking arrays and antennas launch retracted unless the editor's "… extended at launch" is on. A craft
  file or pad save with no `extended` tweak takes the new default. Flight saves from before this change (no
  `panelDeploy` on the part) keep hardware extended as it was flying; `persistence.ts` migrates them on load.
- **Extend above atmosphere** (`autoDeploy` tweak, editor and flight, **on by default**): new players are not punished,
  and the tearing only bites when someone extends by hand. It acts on transitions with hysteresis: extend when
  q < 10 Pa and static pressure < 100 Pa (≈ 40 km on a standard-scale ascent); stow when q > 100 Pa or pressure > 1 kPa.
  A manual extend or retract holds until the air next crosses a threshold. The first evaluation only ever extends, so
  a craft spawned in vacuum opens up and hardware the player set out at launch is not stowed behind their back.
- **Tearing.** Each part has a `maxQ` on its solar/antenna module: Sunfold 3 kPa, Sunwing 2 kPa, Sundial/Solstice
  1.2/1.0 kPa, Thread aerial 6 kPa, Lattice relay 3 kPa, Arcwatch 2 kPa, Horizon 1.2 kPa (3 kPa default for anything
  without one). A stock ascent peaks near 10 kPa, so extended arrays go in the first ~20 s (Meridian: ~600 m, 3.1 kPa);
  slow drifting near the ground (40 m/s ≈ 1 kPa) does not. While moving, the rating halves. An extended part also tears
  when its hottest skin face reaches 80 % of its temperature limit. Stowed and fairing-enclosed hardware is immune.
  A tear goes through `breakJoints` (the part leaves as debris); the `structuralFailure` event carries `cause`, so
  FX/audio/flight log treat it as a breakage, and World posts "<part> was ripped off by the airstream" (or "burned
  through and tore away"). A torn part (`panelTorn`) never extends, generates or links again.
- **Deploy motion is simulated** (`panelDeploy`, 2 s travel); the renderer follows it instead of animating on its own.

### Sea wave clock

- **Cosmetic only:** in multiplayer or a replay, two clients can now show different wave phases after warping. Nothing reads waves for physics.

## Mid-climb GPU: the sky march follows the ray's density (2026-10-13, op/climbgpu)

  rebuilds device resolution; `effectiveRenderScale` (internal / device) is 0.70 with adaptive at ×1.00. The owner's
  pad and mid-climb snapshots (0.70) were at the preset; orbit with the map read 1.00 because the map draws at
  device resolution. The F8 overlay and copied report now show the adaptive multiplier separately
  (`3D scale 0.70, adaptive ×1.00`), so a real reduction is visible as ×0.9 etc.
- **Where the mid-climb GPU time went** (RTX 3060 Ti, Chrome, 3488×2048 canvas = the owner's Mac, Medium, adaptive off,
  Selene paused under the ascent assist at 30 km, material families hidden in turn): sky shell 0.68 ms of 2.35, post
  chain 0.58 (SMAA 0.32, upscale 0.20, bloom 0.17, tone map 0.06), terrain 0.44 (479k tris), ocean 0.23 (261k tris),
  clouds 0.17, glare 0.10, vessel 0.04, shadow map ≤0.03 even when forced to redraw every frame. The sky grows with
  altitude (0.58 → 0.71 ms from 10 to 40 km) because more of the screen is sky and every sky pixel marched 16 steps.
- **Sky steps follow the density at the ray's lowest point.** For rays that do not hit the ground, steps =
  `clamp(ceil(16 · 6 · exp(−h_low / 2H)), 4, 16)` where `h_low` is the altitude of the ray's closest approach within
  the segment and `H = max(Hr, Hm)`. Full 16 steps whenever the ray reaches within ~3.6 scale heights of the
  ground (all rays from the pad and low climb, limb rays from orbit). A numeric check of the shader's integrator
  (Earth, standard and real scale, camera 0.2–110 km, sun −4°…60°, 6,000+ directions) puts the change within
  0.3% of the brightest sky value (≤ 2% relative where the sky is brighter than 5% of that), and it adds no error
  against a 400-step reference beyond the existing 16-step march. Same-frame captures: max 2/255 at 30 km, 1/255
  at 40 km and in orbit. Thick hazes (`uStepScale > 1`: Venus, Titan) keep the full march.
- **Rejected after measuring or checking:** a coarser LOD for chunks fully under deep water (Earth's selected chunks
  at 30 km nearly all contain land or end at the datum, so it never fired); quadratic step placement around the
  perigee (more accurate with fewer steps, but it visibly changes the approved low-altitude sky); terrain Low LOD
  at altitude (−0.26 ms, half the triangles, but visibly coarser relief and per-vertex colour); shadow-map
  changes (≤0.03 ms).
- **F8 per-pass GPU timing.** While the overlay is open, each composer pass has its own timer query (scene incl.
  shadow map, bloom, tone map, SMAA/FXAA, upscale) instead of the frame query; `gpuMs` is their sum and adaptive
  keeps working. The report gains a `GPU passes (ms)` line so a Mac snapshot shows where its time goes. Off when
  the overlay is closed; no per-frame allocation (closures are made once per composer).

## Zen/Firefox ascent at 120 Hz (2026-10-13, op/zenascent)
- **Why Firefox drew ~2× the calls:** Firefox exposes no `WEBGL_multi_draw` (Windows Firefox 143 lists 14 WebGL 2
  extensions, Chrome 31), so three's batched-mesh fallback issued one draw (plus a `_gl_DrawID` uniform) per terrain
  and ocean chunk: 64 of the 136 draws at 10 km, 146 extra on the pad. The rest of the scene is identical.
- **Chunk slot from `gl_VertexID`.** ChunkBatch indices are absolute (slot k = vertices [kV, (k+1)V)), so the chunk
  shaders read the slot as `gl_VertexID / CHUNK_SLOT_VERTS` (define set by ChunkBatch before the first compile)
  instead of three's per-draw indirect lookup. Adjacent visible slots merge into one draw range; the per-frame
  indirect-texture upload is gone (1×1 placeholder, three binds one anyway). Rendering unchanged (Firefox and
  Chrome screenshots, same triangles).
- **Compaction keeps live slots contiguous:** each frame up to 4 of the highest live slots move into the lowest free
  ones (GPU `copyBufferSubData` within the buffer + the 16-float matrix). LOD churn otherwise left 9 terrain ranges
  for 40 chunks. Result: terrain and ocean are one draw each in Firefox, as in Chrome.
- **Triangles: no bug.** At the same canvas and altitude Firefox and Chrome select the same chunks (733,253 tris
  at 10 km, 1600×915). The owner's 1.42 M (Zen) vs 963 k (Chrome) come from canvas height (LOD follows device
  pixels) and altitude (Chrome snapshot at 20–40 km; 10 km is 1.21 M at 3488×2048).
- **Adaptive resolution targets the display rate when uncapped.** It used a fixed 60 fps budget when uncapped, so
  a 120 Hz stream alternating 8.3/16.7 ms (~100 fps) was "within budget" and never reacted — in any browser,
  not because GPU time is n/a in Firefox. Now: the budget is the display interval, estimated from the 20th
  percentile of each window's intervals (a lone short catch-up interval no longer tightens it).
  - Chasing >60 fps never lowers the scale below ×0.75 (`highRateMin`); below 60 fps the full range to ×0.5 applies.
  - Above 60 fps the GPU timer does not decide over/headroom (the Mac's Chrome read 10.2 ms per frame while
    holding 120 fps — Apple timer queries overlap pipelined frames); it still sizes steps and detects CPU-bound frames.
  - After 8 windows over budget at the ×0.75 floor, a 3-window trial at ×1.00 compares steady mean intervals: no
    more than 7% worse → keep ×1.00 and pause reductions (20 s doubling); else back to the floor, next trial after
    30 s doubling to 10 min. Reason: a loading transient during the reduction made the episode check believe it
    helped, stranding ×0.75 where resolution did not matter (seen in Firefox).
  - Owner-visible: on a 120 Hz Mac that misses vsync on GPU load, the 3D resolution now drops (×0.9…×0.75) to hold
    120; if the misses are not pixel-bound (compositor), it tries a reduction, sees no gain and returns to ×1.00,
    with brief retries spaced 20 → 160 s apart. A frame cap of 60 keeps the old behaviour.

## Ascent smoke column (2026-10-13, op/smoke)

- **One chained column, still one draw.** The ascent trail is no longer a row of 30–60 m round cells (which left
  a gap behind the flame at speed, filled the chase camera with a flat grey blob and read as a uniform rope).
  Each trail particle is now a camera-facing ribbon *segment* from its node to the next, younger node; the
  newest segment is re-stretched to the thrust-weighted nozzle centroid every frame, so the column is attached
  and gap-free at any speed or frame time. Nodes are deposited on a time cadence sized to the reserved trail
  region (Medium ≈ 0.27 s, Low ≈ 1.1 s) once the vessel has moved half a column width through the air, so
  hovering does not stack puffs and solar translation is still ignored. All rocket engines feed one column
  (thrust-weighted position, colour and opacity); jets and ion engines do not.
- **Look.** Each end grows with its own age (fast billow-out over ~2 s, then steady spread) and fades as
  1−life²; thin air spreads and dissipates faster (life 90 s at sea level → ~30 s near 0.03 kg/m³) and the
  column fades out between 0.03 and 0.004 kg/m³ (nothing in vacuum; the plume remains). Opacity by
  propellant: solid 1, kerolox .75, methalox .55, hydrolox .35; solids are alumina-white and wider. Smoke is lit
  by the sun colour/intensity (wrap diffuse on a tube/sphere normal, forward scatter at thin edges looking
  sunward) plus a sky ambient that follows daylight, air and a low sun's tint; the engine light's colour glows
  on smoke younger than ~0.2 s, damped in daylight. Billows are 3D value noise (arithmetic hash, no `sin`) in
  the smoke's own frame, seeded per node by its float64 offset from the column's first node (continuous across
  joints, immune to anchor rebasing). Octaves fade by `fwidth` before they alias; end-on segments drop the
  along-axis noise component so stacked caps share one ragged silhouette instead of banding.
- **Joints.** Long segments overlap by 0.6 radius and cross-fade as 1−(1−a)^w, which sums exactly to the
  segment opacity under "over" blending (a linear cross-fade left a visible thin band). Short/end-on segments
  get round caps; a window guarantees zero alpha at every quad border (no hard cuts).
- **Overdraw bound.** Segment ends are clipped to ≥ 2 radii in front of the eye and fade from 4 to 2 radii, so no
  particle can fill the screen when the camera sits in or behind the column (old cells did). Uploads use
  separate dirty ranges for the burst and trail regions; the only per-frame upload is the head segment.
- **Cost.** CDP Chrome (RTX 3060 Ti, 2940×1840): draws unchanged (one particle draw); scene pass ascent
  1.841→1.827 ms, climb10 1.914→1.896, climb20 1.48→1.478 (noise). Particle draw cost (fenced, shown minus
  hidden) worst view 0.30 ms before / 0.31 ms after, chase 0.04–0.07 ms both. Firefox ASAP climb, medium,
  scale 1: mean frame interval 9.38/8.92 ms before vs 9.73/7.26 ms after (run-to-run noise), `fx` CPU section
  ~0.1 ms both. No per-frame allocation (`trailStyle` writes into a reused object). Scripts:
  `scripts/bench/smoke-shots.mjs` (checkpoint screenshots), `scripts/bench/smoke/{cost,pool}.js` (gpu-passes
  `--eval`). Pad liftoff billows, ground dust and pad steam are unchanged apart from the new lighting.

## Career playthrough fixes (2026-10-13, op/career)

- **Parachutes open at their rated load, then stay reefed until slow.** An armed chute used to open at 1.5× its
  rated dynamic pressure, which is exactly the cut threshold, so on a fast descent it opened and was cut on the
  next step. It now opens at ≤ 1× `maxQ` and goes from reefed to full only below `deployAltitude` **and** ≤ 0.2×
  `maxQ` (a full canopy at the reefed load tore the chute off its joint). Cut stays at 1.5×. No part ratings
  changed. Measured: the stock Kestrel flown straight up at full throttle (420 km apex) went from crew lost
  to recovered.
- **Capsules trim heat-shield first.** Aerodynamic forces act at part centres, so nothing gave a capsule its
  real blunt-body stability: every capsule tumbled to apex-first, fell at ~250 m/s and hit the sea before the chute
  could open (SAS held it only until ~65 kPa). A heat shield whose axial face leads now carries a blunt-face drag
  along the flow through its sphere centre, 2 shield radii behind the face (`BLUNT_CENTRE`; real capsules ≈ 2.4),
  blended in between 30° and 70° off the axis. A spent stack decoupler left under the shield (decouplers stay with
  the upper stage) is folded into the shield's face for drag only (`cache.aeroExposed`, built at topology
  rebuild; heating still uses `exposed`). No per-step cost beyond one module check per part. Result: the Kestrel
  flown straight up (315 km apex) with SAS off comes down shield-first and lands under its chute.

  uncrewed craft could circularise or fly a TLI (the same holds for any probe in low orbit on every scale, e.g. the
  Ringrunner Real). Rule now: the maneuver nodes a vessel has while it has command authority are *uploaded*; out of
  contact, a node-execution program (Flight assist → Execute next node, or the headless `executeNode`) flying an

  uploaded until the next contact (the planner shows its index in amber with an explanation). Mechanism:
  `ControlInput.program` (node id) set by `executeNode`, `World.uploadedNode`, authority check in `World.tick`/
  `physicsStep`; the flight scene keeps such an assist running on signal loss. Not saved: after a reload out of
  contact the probe waits for the next pass. Light-time delay is still not modelled.
- **Entry trim** (maneuver planner, worker operation `entry`, `game/planning/entry.ts`). The button appears when the
  trajectory (after any planned nodes) comes down into an atmosphere: an arrival from another sphere of influence or
  an eccentric return with periapsis below 2× the atmosphere height, or any orbit whose periapsis is in the air (a
  parking orbit is not an entry). Corridor model: a point mass flying the conic's energy and angular momentum through
  the body's atmosphere with drag only, using the vessel's hypersonic C_d·A/m from the flight aero model in a
  retrograde attitude, measured on a copy with every remaining stage fired (what enters is the capsule, not the
  stack). Skip edge = highest periapsis that still gets deep; steep edge = 10 g peak. No fitted constants: it matches
  the rigid-body integrator's skip edge for the stock capsule within 0.5 km (Standard/Challenging/Real 23.8/38.0/
  59.1 km vs measured 24.2/38.3/~60) and peak g within ~10%. The planned point is the 25 kPa lunar-return design
  pressure, clamped to [steep + 25 %, shallow − 15 %] of the corridor: a 29 kPa point (25 % below the skip edge, the
  first version) overheated the Meridian's side-mounted thermometer and lost 49 science on the Standard flyby;
  heating itself is not modelled. Burn: minimum-norm Newton solve over prograde/normal/radial at three candidate
  times (two minutes after the last existing node, ten minutes into the arrival sphere, the return leg's apoapsis);
  the cheapest wins; existing nodes are kept and flown first. The card shows Δv, a corridor gauge (steep · safe ·
  skip, current periapsis hollow, planned solid) and the timeline. ~0.25 s in the worker (Chrome and Firefox).
- Measured: Moon returns 15 km too shallow / 8 km too steep need 0.4–1.3 m/s; flown with the real integrator they
  capture in one pass on every scale with hypersonic peak 3.3 / 4.7 / 7.8 g (Standard / Challenging / Real). The
  8 g reading after that on Standard is the parachute opening below 1 km/s, unrelated.

## Stale build after a service-worker update (2026-10-14)

- **Bug:** opening a production build after a rebuild ran the old cached build; the auto-updating service worker
  (skipWaiting + clientsClaim) took over seconds later and dropped the old hashed chunks, so the next lazy scene
  (Integration Hall, Tracking, ...) failed with "Failed to fetch dynamically imported module" and the crash screen.
  Reproduced headlessly: build, load, rebuild, reload, wait, enter the Integration Hall.
- **Fix** (`src/ui/staleBuild.ts`): when a replacement worker takes control while the player is on the main menu,
  reload at once; elsewhere, a failed chunk (`vite:preloadError`) reloads the page onto the new build (back to the
  main menu) instead of crashing. The error boundary renders nothing while that reload runs. A second chunk
  failure within 10 s of such a reload shows the crash screen (offline or broken deploy, not a stale build).
  Mid-flight scenes never auto-reload on the worker change alone.

## Launch region terrain: Florida height patch and footprint plateau (2026-10-14, op/capefix)

- **Regional heights:** `public/earth/height-cape.i16` holds native ETOPO 2022 15 arc-second pixels (~460 m) for

  `height.i16` into the one buffer `installEarthData` already takes, so the worker / SharedArrayBuffer path is

## Map zoom and focus are predictable (2026-10-15, op/mapzoom)

Owner, in Zen on the Mac: zooming the map "starts panning and twirling", from orbit "the whole globe flips around",
and Focus on Earth "doesn't save when I go out of the map and back in".
- **Cause.** Two rules from op/mapbiomes keyed on zoom distance alone: below ~4 R the camera's yaw/pitch blended from
  inertial angles into the vessel's local-horizon frame, and below 3 R the follow focus slid from the body's centre to
  the vessel. From low orbit both fired while the wheel turned. Headless reproduction (`tests/map-zoom.test.ts` on
  main): a 65° camera turn in one frame. CDP Chrome on the prod build: 139° of total turn, 34° in one frame,
  azimuth −146° → +38°.
- **Rule now: zoom changes distance only, except towards the cursor.** Yaw/pitch are inertial at every zoom. When the
  camera looks at a body's centre (Earth focused, or following a vessel in orbit) the wheel zooms towards the ground
  under the cursor, like a map app: the point picked when the wheel turns stays under the cursor while the zoom eases
  (`pickSphere`/`aimAt` in `render/map/framing.ts`, two or three Newton steps a frame while easing, no allocation).
  Cursor off the globe or at the centre: a straight zoom. Revert or refine: refined. A plain revert would have lost
  the close regional view (Florida, cities, biomes); this keeps it with no automatic re-centring.
- **How close.** Body-centred views stop 0.1 R above solid ground (was 1.15 R from the centre for a focused body,
  0.12 R from a low vessel); 0.15 R above gas giants. Close in, a drag turns the globe about as fast as the ground
  under the cursor moves (rate ∝ altitude, capped at the old 0.005 rad/px), instead of sweeping hundreds of km.
  The orbit-line fade near the ground now keys on the camera's height above the reference body.
- **Kept:** the ascent view (owner's PR #6): from the pad until orbit the map centres between vessel and apoapsis,
  which is how a landed vessel gets its close regional view (Florida and its cities). It still reframes once when
  orbit is reached. One change: its opening angles looked from the north side of the orbit plane, so with the
  horizon frame gone the pad view opened with north at the bottom (Boston below Miami). `ascentAngles` now looks
  from the side away from the body's north pole and ~35° above the vessel's horizon (was ~17°), so north is up.
- **Evidence.** Driver `C:\wsp-tools\drv\mapzoom.mjs` (CDP Chrome) and `C:\wsp-tools\drv-ff\mapzoom-ff.mjs`
  (Firefox), orbit scenario, 40 wheel steps with the cursor on the globe; shots in `C:\wsp-tools\shots\mapzoom\`.
  Largest one-frame camera turn, main → branch: Chrome 34° → 0.7°, Firefox 64° → 1.6°; the ground first under the
  cursor drifts 635 → 23 px (Chrome) and 512 → 16 px (Firefox; Earth turns under the inertial camera meanwhile).
  Focus on Earth after close/reopen: null on main, kept on the branch. Regression tests: `tests/map-zoom.test.ts`
  (fails on main with a 65° one-frame turn). Per-frame cost: the horizon-frame maths is gone; while a zoom eases,
  `aimAt` adds at most 18 small trig evaluations. Not benchmarked.
- **Focus persistence.** A focus the player picks (Focus on Earth, a moon, another vessel) and its view (yaw, pitch,
  distance) now survive closing and reopening the map for the flight scene's lifetime. Not saved with the game: the
  map's camera is not part of the world snapshot, and a reload starts by following the vessel. Home (or Frame active
  orbit) returns to following the vessel, which reframes its orbit on every reopen as before. A focus that no longer
  exists (vessel gone) falls back to following.

## Earth biomes from measured land cover (2026-10-15, op/landcover)

- **Source.** Earth cover classes come from NASA MODIS MCD12Q1 v6.1 IGBP 2023 (NASA data, no restrictions), fetched as
  eight 0.025° GIBS WMS tiles and decoded exactly through the GIBS colormap (no Earthdata login needed). ESA CCI was
  avoided (CC-BY). Natural Earth 10m lakes (PD) mark lakes; the Caspian (sea in Natural Earth) is flood-filled from
  MODIS water. Inputs are pinned by URL + SHA-256 in `scripts/earth-data/cover.mjs`; a rebuild is byte-identical.
- **Florida uses USGS NLCD 2021 (public domain) inside the `CAPE_PATCH` bounds.** At 500 m MODIS calls Merritt Island
  and most Florida marsh savanna or water, so the pad had no wetlands. NLCD maps woody/emergent wetlands at 30 m;
  the ring 3 playable km from the pad is now mostly Wetlands on all three scales.
- **Grid.** 2880×1440 (0.125°, ~14 km), 4-bit classes, 2.07 MB, appended to the one Earth buffer as a third
  `EARTH_FILES` entry, so workers, SharedArrayBuffer, Node register and the server need no new plumbing (+2 MB per
  non-isolated worker, +2 MB first download; PWA precaches `*.u4`). Votes: 5×5 MODIS samples per cell, permanent
  wetlands weighted 2× (they are a mosaic with water at 500 m); latitude splits rainforest (evergreen broadleaf
  < 30°) and tundra (open shrub ≥ 55°, grass/barren ≥ 60°); boreal (savanna) woodland ≥ 50° is Forests; crops and
  urban are Grasslands. Sea cells take the nearest land class, then two 5×5 majority passes and a minimum region of
  40 cells (8 for lakes and wetlands, so Lake Okeechobee survives; tested). The first in-game globe shot with 3×3 /
  8 cells still read as forest/grassland speckle over Europe and Siberia.
- **Biome rules.** Lakes (new) > deep sea > Antarctica/peaks > 5500 m ice > Oceans > measured ice > Wetlands (new)
  > Shores > Mountains (> 2200 m) > measured class. The latitude snowline no longer makes science ice: it made the
  whole Tibetan plateau "Ice Caps"; it is now Mountains. The snowline stays as a render-only colour rule, so ground
  snow looks unchanged. Old hand-placed ellipses and latitude bands are gone.
- **Saves/contracts.** All ten previous Earth IDs keep their names and order; `Wetlands`, `Lakes` are appended, so
  archives, decay keys, onboard data and accepted contracts stay valid. Contract biome picks for a given seed change
  (list length grew); stored contracts keep their biome string. Every Earth biome is reachable (tested).
- **Terrain colour.** `earthPreset.colorAt` blends the four nearest cells' palette colours bilinearly instead of
  hard class edges (a step would be a 1.4 km straight colour edge on Standard). New Wetlands/Lakes colours are dark
  olive. Seen from altitude the imagery dominates as before.
- **Cost.** `earthCover` in-process paired: 42.6 → 42.7 ns random points, 34.9 → 33.1 ns spatially coherent; deep
  sea skips the lookup. Cross-process `biomeAt` and chunk-build runs were within noise (machine load 16–19).
  Earth biome-map build 6.3 → 5.3 s in Node.

## Worker failures and flight load (2026-10-15, op/workerfix)
- **Owner crash ("Planning worker failed" / terrain worker overlay, Zen on macOS).** The report's frames name
  `src/…` files with esbuild line numbers (no sourcemaps in prod), so the owner ran the Vite dev server. The planner
  text is the message-less `onerror` branch, which Firefox fires only when a worker *script fails to load*. Exceptions
  inside the async terrain `onmessage` become rejections and never reach `Worker.onerror` (checked in Firefox).
  Blocking `planner.worker`/`chunkWorker` in real Firefox reproduces both owner messages on main ("Planning worker
  failed", "Flight interrupted"). Why the dev server failed to serve the scripts (restart, mid-pull file, network) is
  not provable from here.
- **Recovery rather than crash.** A terrain worker error terminates that worker and rejects its requests; the quadtree
  and the far bake already retry rejections. The worker respawns with the Earth grid and every registered terrain.
  There are 3 respawns per session in total; after that, failing slots retire, and with none left the pool builds on
  the main thread (`available` is now derived). The flight shows a warning message (`wsp:worker-warning`), and the
  crash overlay no longer listens for worker errors. Request-level worker exceptions now come back with their
  request ID and stack instead of leaving a promise pending forever. A planning worker that fails to load plans
  on the main thread through the existing in-process handler; worker exceptions that carry a message are still
  reported. The crash report now includes the error message, because Firefox's `Error.stack` lists frames only.
- **Flight load: bakes no longer block chunk builds.** The pool sent chunk builds to the least-loaded worker, which

- **Sim/editor fixes the ion probe exposed:** the stage Δv readout treats massless propellant (electric charge) as
  resupplied, so ion stages show their xenon-limited Δv instead of ~2 m/s; the editor's "no fuel" check accepts
  vessel-wide resources (xenon, monopropellant, charge) from any part, not just ancestors; an engine short only of

## Career past the Moon: Venus and Mars (2026-10-15, op/career2)
- **Petrel Planetary Probe** (`src/editor/stock/probes.ts`; `stock-petrel`, `-challenging`, `-real`). Before it, no
  stock craft could return science from Mars or Venus on any scale below 640–1,170 science of research: Tern's Thread
  aerial reaches 2 Gm (the Moon, not the planets), Cinderbloom's Lattice dish leaves with its cruise stage, Duskwing
  (Challenging) needs 1,170, Real had nothing. Petrel = the Tern bus with an Arcwatch dish (Earth link 200 Gm) on the
  scale's Meridian launcher; the Wisp kick stage stays aboard for corrections and capture. Research: the scale orbiter,
  Remote Navigation and Long Range Telemetry (Standard 200, Challenging/Real 450). Fits level-1 pad and hall on every
  scale. The Real variant carries a second kick tank and a longer upper stage (+0.8 km/s past orbit) so it captures at
  Venus; a Real Mars capture still needs ~1.35–1.7 km/s it lacks, and the Arcwatch reaches Real Mars only near close
  approach. A Horizon dish costs the small probe 0.5–0.8 km/s: Real Mars orbit is left to player designs.
- **Escape burns keep their guidance** (`autopilot.ts`, `GUIDE_MAX_ANGLE_ESCAPE` 150°). The node executor aimed its
  Lambert guidance up to 175° around the planned orbit; on an escape hyperbola with a departure node before periapsis
  that point is nearly opposite the burn, and the small out-of-plane offset of a plane-change burn flipped the Lambert
  plane: a 1.4 km/s Venus departure (Flight assist → Execute next node, or headless) stopped after 359 m/s. Escape plans
  now aim at most 150° ahead; elliptical plans (Moon transfers) are unchanged.
- **"Go now" interplanetary plans cost what the options list says** (`interplanetary.ts`). Planning the next-orbit
  option scored each grid cell by the cheapest ejection within ±half an orbit of its date, partly in the past, then
  searched the burn time only near that date: the list priced "now" at 2.2 km/s and the plan came out at 3.6–5.4 km/s
  depending on the minute (and the planner's own default was sometimes that plan). Both now use the reachable next orbit.
- **Course corrections from solar orbit keep the periapsis above the air** (`planning/arrival.ts`). "Transfer → Mars"
  while cruising (the planner's route for a mid-course fix) aimed at half the atmosphere height (31 km at Standard
  Mars, inside the air); it now uses the departure planner's safe arrival (max of 0.1 R, 2 × atmosphere height, 30 km).
